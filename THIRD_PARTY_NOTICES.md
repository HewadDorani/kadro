# Third-party data and media

Kandro shows nutrition values that come from named external sources. Two of them
oblige us to credit them in the product itself, not only here: the app links to
these notices under **Du → Datenquellen**.

## Bundeslebensmittelschlüssel (BLS) 4.0

Curated German composite dishes use reference values from the BLS.

> Max Rubner-Institut (2025): Bundeslebensmittelschlüssel (BLS), Version 4.0 –
> Deutsche Nährstoffdatenbank. Karlsruhe.
> DOI: 10.25826/Data20251217-134202-0

- License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)
- Source: https://blsdb.de/download
- Attribution is **required**. The Max Rubner-Institut neither reviewed nor
  endorsed Kandro; values were selected and scaled to portion sizes by us.

## USDA FoodData Central

Individual ingredients outside the German dish reference are matched against
FoodData Central, U.S. Department of Agriculture.

- Public domain (work of the U.S. federal government); citation requested,
  attribution not legally required.
- Source: https://fdc.nal.usda.gov

## Open Food Facts

Barcode lookups resolve against the Open Food Facts product database.

- License: Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0
- Source: https://world.openfoodfacts.org
- Attribution is **required** for the data we display.

The Plan → Supermarkt suggestions use a bundled extract of Open Food Facts
product records (`src/data/supermarketProducts.json`, generated by
`scripts/build-supermarket-products.mjs`; name, brand, stores tag, package size,
serving and nutrients per 100 g of roughly 300 products sold in Germany).

- The extract is a derivative database of Open Food Facts and is licensed
  under the ODbL 1.0 (share-alike); individual contents are under the Database
  Contents License 1.0. Anyone who receives it may reuse it under those terms.
- The app is a Produced Work: it shows "Produktdaten: Open Food Facts (ODbL)"
  with the data date under the suggestions, and every logged product keeps its
  barcode as source.
- Because the extract ships inside the app, the ODbL requires that the
  extract (or the build script that recreates it) is available under the ODbL.
  It is published openly at `https://getkandro.com/data/supermarket-products.json`
  (`site/data/`, with `LICENSE.txt`); keep that copy in sync with
  `src/data/supermarketProducts.json` whenever the data is rebuilt.
- Product data, store listings and recipes can change; the extract records
  its fetch date and is refreshed by rerunning the build script.

Product search and barcode lookups first query a server-side product index,
`public.products_de`: products sold in Germany from the Open Food Facts
database export (name, brands, stores tags, package and serving size,
nutrients per 100 g or per 100 ml, main category, Nutri-Score, scan count).
It is built by `scripts/build-products-de.py` (filters, plausibility checks
and normalisation) and loaded by `scripts/load-products-de.mjs`; live Open
Food Facts answers whatever it does not contain.

- `products_de` is a derivative database of Open Food Facts under the ODbL
  1.0 (share-alike); contents under the Database Contents License 1.0. It
  holds no user data.
- Search and barcode results shown in the app are Produced Works: each keeps
  "Open Food Facts <barcode>" as its source.
- Because the index is publicly used through the app, we offer the derivative
  database under the ODbL: the build script above is the method that recreates
  it from the public export, and the built file (`products-de.ndjson.gz`) is
  provided on request or published alongside the supermarket extract
  (`https://getkandro.com/data/`) whenever it is rebuilt.
